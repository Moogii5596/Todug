# Content-format, engagement and business-model patterns in parent-facing child-development / micro-learning products (as of Nov 2026)

> Method note for the report writer: this session's network allowed web *search* but blocked direct page fetches (nesta.org.uk, pubs.asha.org, blogs.worldbank.org, edweek.org, kinedu.com, mb-cdi.stanford.edu, huckleberry.zendesk.com all failed to resolve). Claims that come only from company marketing are tagged **[marketing]**. Cost/complexity ratings (S / M / L for a 1-3 person team) are my inference, not sourced.

## Which lesson formats get parents to actually do the activity daily?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced format is not an app lesson at all but a tiny push message: 3 short texts a week structured FACT → TIP → GROWTH (why it matters, one concrete thing to do inside an existing routine, encouragement/extension), which raised parent home-literacy activity by 0.15–0.34 SD and child literacy by ~0.11 SD; the app-based "library of activities" format (EasyPeasy) produced no overall language gain in a large independent trial. Activities embedded in play/daily routines out-perform separate sessions in the parent-implemented language-intervention literature (g ≈ 0.50 for expressive vocabulary), and goal-setting + reminders + feedback roughly doubled parent activity time in the one trial that isolated those behavioural tools.

### Cited Findings

**Pattern 1 — FACT / TIP / GROWTH micro-messages (Ready4K → ParentPowered; Tips-by-Text; Bright by Text is the same genre)**
- (a/b) Ready4K: 8-month SMS programme for parents of preschoolers; each week covers one skill with three texts — a "FACT" message to build buy-in and a "TIP" message with a practical activity (plus a third follow-up/"growth" text). Trial in San Francisco Unified 2013–14. — [NBER w20659](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20659.pdf); [Annenberg/Brown summary](https://annenberg.brown.edu/publications/one-step-time-effects-early-literacy-text-messaging-program-parents-preschoolers)
- (c) Published result (York, Loeb & Doss, J. Human Resources 2019): parental involvement at home and school up 0.15–0.29 SD; child early-literacy gains ~0.11 SD. Working-paper version reports home literacy engagement +0.22–0.34 SD. — [JHR PDF](https://jhr.uwpress.org/content/54/3/537.full.pdf); [NBER W20659](https://www.nber.org/papers/W20659)
- (c) A later version reports children below the baseline median gained 0.31 SD in literacy (p<0.01) — i.e., the format helps the children who are behind the most. — [CEPA working paper 2017](https://cepa.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/One%20Step%20CEPA%20Working%20Paper%206%201%2017.pdf)
- (c) Dosage experiment (Cortes, Fricke, Loeb & Song, 2018; Education Finance and Policy 2021): one text/week was not enough for lower-performing children; five/week added nothing over three and was less effective when it carried more actionable advice; three per week was the preferred model. — [IZA DP 11669 / EFP 2021](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/11669/imprint); [Annenberg brief "Too Little or Too Much"](https://annenberg.brown.edu/sites/default/files/TBT_Brief_Too%20Little_or_Too_Much.pdf); [EdWeek coverage](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/more-isnt-always-better-in-texting-preschool-parents-study-finds/2018/07)
- (c) Opt-out rises with volume and complexity: "It's too annoying" (Fricke, Kalogrides & Loeb) found more texts and more complex texts produced more opt-outs, while adding context and encouragement alongside the activity reduced opt-out. One account gives 8% opt-out at 5 texts/week vs 5% at 3 (verify — sources differ on exact rates). — [Annenberg](https://annenberg.brown.edu/publications/its-too-annoying-who-drops-out-educational-text-messaging-programs-and-why); [CEPA](https://cepa.stanford.edu/node/2133); [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/more-isnt-always-better-in-texting-preschool-parents-study-finds/2018/07)
- (d) Cost/complexity: **S**. Pure text; the content pattern transfers directly to web-push, Messenger/Viber or SMS.

**Pattern 2 — Weekly text linking to a 1–3 min modelling video (BBC Tiny Happy People text service, with Nesta)**
- (a/b) Parents of 6–24-month-olds get roughly one text a week for up to 18 months, each linking to a short evidence-informed Tiny Happy People video of an activity to do with the child. — [Nesta project page](https://nesta.org.uk/project/bbc-tiny-happy-people-text-messaging-programme)
- (c) RCT led by Danielle Matthews: parents who received the language-learning videos were more likely to have improved linguistic responsiveness than controls; the registry entry ISRCTN45990335 is listed completed with results not yet posted, so the full paper was not found. — [Nesta report](https://www.nesta.org.uk/report/engaging-parents-digitally-learning-from-the-tiny-happy-people-text-messaging-service/full/); [Matthews presentation](https://www.york.ac.uk/media/healthsciences/documents/trials/Danielle%20Matthews%20Presentation.pdf); [ISRCTN](https://www.isrctn.com/pdf/45990335)
- (c) Pilot Jan–Mar 2025: 28% of website visitors signed up; 1,763 sign-ups; ~70% of the 186 survey respondents rated texts useful/very useful (low response rate — weak signal). Sign-ups skewed to less-deprived areas, but once enrolled, parents in deprived deciles clicked video links at a similar rate. — [Nesta report – access & engagement](https://www.nesta.org.uk/report/engaging-parents-digitally-learning-from-the-tiny-happy-people-test-messaging-service/increasing-access-and-engagement-with-the-programme)
- (d) Nesta estimates ~£3.05 per parent for the whole programme including hosting and reply texts. — [Nesta report](https://www.nesta.org.uk/report/engaging-parents-digitally-learning-from-the-tiny-happy-people-text-messaging-service/full/). Complexity **S** for delivery; **L** for producing the videos (BBC had them already).

**Pattern 3 — In-app library of game/activity cards with weekly nudges (EasyPeasy, UK)**
- (c) EEF efficacy trial (~1,500 children aged 3–4, 139 nursery settings): no additional months' progress on the composite language score; ~1 month gain on "word structure" and "concepts and following directions" subscales; exploratory +2 months for boys in Communication & Language; some longer-term effect on "Good Level of Development"; three-padlock security with baseline imbalance. — [EEF project page](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/easypeasy-learning-through-play); [EEF news](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/easypeasy-intervention-shows-promise-in-supporting-aspects-of-childrens-early-development)
- (c) Earlier small Newham trial found a positive effect on parent-reported cognitive self-regulation; small sample. — [Sutton Trust](https://www.suttontrust.com/news-opinion/all-news-opinion/easypeasy-parenting-app-help-toddlers-develop/)
- (d) **M** (content library + nudges). Evidence says a library alone does not move language.

**Pattern 4 — "Brain-building" tips that need no time, money or equipment and state the science (Vroom; Thrive by Five "Collective Actions")**
- (a/b) Vroom tips are designed to fit existing moments, require no extra time/money/equipment, and each explains the underlying science ("Brainy Background"). — [Washington DOH description](https://www.doh.wa.gov/CommunityandEnvironment/EssentialsforChildhoodInitiative/BrainBuildingwithVroom)
- (c) Evidence is thin: Vroom's own reported results (awareness, confidence) are self-reported **[marketing]**; the one RCT found (Evansville, 12 classrooms, bundled with Mind in the Making and Circle Time Games) is an unpublished exploratory draft reporting executive-function and maths gains. — [Vroom](https://vroom.org/support/); [CT ESSA evidence guide PDF](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/SDE/ESSA-Evidence-Guides/Outcome_Evaluation_of_Mind_in_the_Making_Vroom_and_Circle_Time_Games)
- (a/b) Thrive by Five (Minderoo): each "Collective Action" pairs a piece of science with a child-rearing activity for parents, extended family and community. — [Framework paper, PMC9972208](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9972208)
- (c) Published work is protocols, qualitative studies (Indonesia, 57 stakeholders) and a 10-LMIC multicase study stressing strong local partners; no outcome results on caregiver behaviour were found. — [Digital Health 2024 protocol](https://doaj.org/article/655bbdf149d9492abe5182ec81e77eb1); [JMIR 2025](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e65705)
- (d) **S** — the "why + do" two-part card is text-native.

**Pattern 5 — Personalised daily plan of short video activities (Kinedu, BabySparks, Lovevery app)**
- (a/b) Kinedu: personalised daily plan; premium = 3,000+ video activities, expert-led classes, progress reports and unlimited questions to an AI assistant. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id741277284)
- (a/b) BabySparks: personalised daily programme for ages 0–3 across cognition, language, motor, social-emotional, sensory, self-care; each activity has a brief instructional video; activities are tied to milestones. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/babysparks-development-app/id794574199)
- (a/b) Lovevery app: daily baby/toddler activities, tips and expert insight by stage, bundled with Play Kits. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1556748552)
- (c) No independent outcome or retention data found for any of the three. Kinedu cites Stanford researchers analysing data from 21,861 children (about skill structure, not app efficacy) **[marketing]**. — [Kinedu science page](https://www.kinedu.com/science)
- (c) User-side friction signals: BabySparks reviewers complain about being asked to complete 5 activities a day and about intrusive prompts. — [App Store reviews via listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/babysparks-development-app/id794574199)
- (d) **L** — thousands of filmed activities is the moat; not replicable without budget.

**Pattern 6 — Routine-embedded strategies rather than separate "sessions" (EMT, Hanen-style parent-implemented intervention)**
- (b) Enhanced Milieu Teaching embeds language-building opportunities in routine everyday activities; sessions took place at home during play, book reading, snack and a household task or routine. — [Roberts & Kaiser 2015, Pediatrics](https://ei.northwestern.edu/files/2023/05/peds_20163646.pdf); [JSLHR 2021](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2021_JSLHR-20-00430)
- (c) Heidlage et al. 2020 meta-analysis (25 RCTs, children 0–8): expressive-vocabulary effect was significant for shared book reading (g = 0.37, 95% CI 0.15–0.59) and for interventions implemented in play and/or routines (g = 0.50, 95% CI 0.05–0.95). Overall expressive effect is reported as g = 0.42 in one summary and g = 0.27 in another (verify against the paper); receptive effects are small (g = 0.07–0.18). — [ASHA Evidence Map summary](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/2df79249-9828-4a65-9c01-ff8a849e7a39); [Northwestern EI lab PDF](https://ei.northwestern.edu/files/2023/04/Heidlage-et-al-2019-The-effects-of-parent-implemented-interventions.pdf)
- (c) A 2024 JSLHR EMT systematic review/meta-analysis snippet states EMT was more effective when implemented by caregivers than by therapists (verify; could not open). — [JSLHR 2024](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2024_JSLHR-24-00260)
- (c) Roberts & Kaiser 2015 RCT, 97 toddlers with language delay: positive immediate effect on receptive language; primary outcome not blinded. — [Pediatrics PDF](https://ei.northwestern.edu/files/2023/05/peds_20163646.pdf)
- (d) **S** — re-tagging each lesson to a routine (bath, meal, dressing, walk) is a content decision, not an engineering one.

**Pattern 7 — Goal-setting + daily reminder + weekly feedback + recognition (PACT, Chicago)**
- (b) Parents set a weekly reading-time goal on a website, received a text every weekday about the goal, saw actual-vs-goal at week end, got a congratulatory text if they met it and a weekly anonymous "top reader" social-recognition message. — [J-PAL summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/node/2124); [NBER w21602](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21602.pdf)
- (c) Over six weeks treated parents read 88.3 more minutes and 16.6 more books — more than double the control group; effect largest for the most present-biased (least patient) parents. — [UChicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/texts-and-tablets-more-double-time-parents-spend-reading-kids); [J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/node/2124)
- (d) **S–M** — a weekly "how many days will you practise?" prompt, a daily nudge, and an end-of-week comparison are simple CRUD + scheduled messages.

**Pattern 8 — Chatbot-delivered modular programme on a messaging app (ParentText, UNICEF/Parenting for Lifelong Health, Oxford)**
- (a/b) ParentText is a chatbot-led digital version of the in-person PLH programmes delivered on platforms such as WhatsApp. — [Oxford DSPI](https://www.spi.ox.ac.uk/parenttext-optimisation-evaluation-south-africa)
- (c) Engagement is the recurring obstacle: Oxford describes engagement as a significant barrier in early pilots; users commonly exited before seeing all the material. — [Oxford DSPI](https://www.spi.ox.ac.uk/parenttext-optimisation-evaluation-south-africa); [BMC Public Health 2023](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1186/s12889-023-16649-w)
- (b) The optimisation factorial trial (Malaysia, South Africa; NCT05003518) tests personalisation (high/low), gender-targeted messages, gamified module-completion updates and frequency (1 vs 3 interactions/day). Results were not found as of this search. — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05003518); [JMIR Res Protoc 2024](https://www.researchprotocols.org/2024/1/e52145/XML)
- (c) A 2026 mixed-methods optimisation study (Jamaica, Malaysia, South Africa) identified 23 engagement barriers — including high message burden, onboarding design, and platform policies restricting follow-up after non-response — and 29 optimisations, 69% rated highly feasible. — [Oxford publication record](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/publication/2447695)
- (c) A 2026 Malaysia evaluation reported improved positive parenting and reduced child externalising behaviour (from search summary; verify). — [Oxford DSPI](https://www.spi.ox.ac.uk/parenttext-optimisation-evaluation-south-africa)
- (d) **M** — needs a WhatsApp/Messenger business integration and flow tooling; note the platform-policy barrier (cannot message non-responders after the window closes).

**Pattern 9 — Group classes + objective feedback report (LENA Start)**
- (b) 13-week parent group programme; families make up to 12 day-long recordings and review feedback (adult words, conversational turns) in the group sessions. — [J. Child Language 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32921333/)
- (c) Non-randomised preliminary evaluation (39 LENA Start dyads vs 17 comparison): 72% met graduation criteria; small-to-medium effects favouring LENA Start, significant for child vocalisations and conversational turns, not adult word count. — [Cambridge Core](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-child-language/article/abs/parent-education-to-improve-early-language-development-a-preliminary-evaluation-of-lena-starttm/9ABEDE26C14B4677DC8D2E2373EEB27A)
- (d) **L** (hardware) — but the principle "show the parent a number about *their own talk/turns* each week" is portable as self-report.

**Pattern 10 — Health SMS by gestational/child age (Text4baby)**
- (c) RCT at Madigan Army Medical Center (943 randomised): short-term effects on targeted attitudes and beliefs; in the dose-response analysis the only significant behavioural effect was lower postpartum alcohol use among high-exposure participants (OR 0.212, 95% CI 0.046–0.973). — [JMIR mHealth 2015](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2015/1/e12/); [PMC4327187](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4327187)

### Inferences
- The formats with causal evidence share four properties: (i) one idea at a time, (ii) the activity is attached to a moment that already happens, (iii) it is *pushed* to the parent rather than waiting in a library, (iv) the "why" precedes the "do". Todug's current lesson (3 steps + script) has (i) but not necessarily (ii)–(iv).
- "Daily" is probably the wrong push cadence. The only head-to-head dosage evidence favours ~3 touches/week over 1 or 5; ParentText lists message burden as a barrier. A defensible design is: one strategy per week, practised daily inside a named routine, with 3 nudges (why / do / how did it go + extension).
- EasyPeasy's null result and ParentText's attrition both suggest that content quality is not the bottleneck — delivery, timing and feedback are.
- PACT is the strongest argument for adding an implementation-intention step ("When will you do this? After bath / at breakfast…") plus a weekly goal-vs-actual recap to the existing tick-off mechanic.

### Gaps
- No retention or completion data (D7/D30, activity-completion rates) is published by Kinedu, BabySparks, Lovevery, Huckleberry, Tinybeans or The Wonder Weeks; none was found in independent sources.
- ParentText optimisation-trial effect sizes for frequency/gamification/personalisation: not found (South Africa registration reportedly terminated; analysis "underway").
- Tiny Happy People full RCT paper and the Nesta click-through-by-week data could not be opened (host blocked).
- Bright by Text, Tinybeans, The Wonder Weeks, Huckleberry and WHO/UNICEF Care for Child Development were not individually researched within the call budget; no claims are made about them here.
- No RCT found that compares routine-embedded vs separate-session delivery head-to-head for late talkers; the g = 0.50 figure is a subgroup estimate with a wide CI.

## What is the smallest effective "unit" of content, and how long should it be?

### Takeaway
The smallest unit with trial evidence is a single SMS-length message (one fact or one tip), grouped as a three-message weekly set on one skill; more complex or more numerous messages increase drop-out. Where video is used, the evidenced unit is one short film per week, not per day.

### Cited Findings
- Ready4K's unit is one text; the weekly set is three texts on a single skill (fact, tip, follow-up). — [NBER w20659](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20659.pdf)
- More complex texts and higher quantity increased opt-out; context and encouragement reduced it. — [Annenberg](https://annenberg.brown.edu/publications/its-too-annoying-who-drops-out-educational-text-messaging-programs-and-why)
- Five texts carrying more actionable advice were less effective than three. — [IZA DP 11669](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/11669/imprint)
- Tiny Happy People's unit is one text + one short video about once a week for up to 18 months. — [Nesta](https://nesta.org.uk/project/bbc-tiny-happy-people-text-messaging-programme)
- ParentText tested 1 vs 3 interactions per day and later identified "high message burden" as an engagement barrier. — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05003518); [Oxford](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/publication/2447695)
- A separate 2025 text-messaging trial found no significant difference in click rates between high- and low-dose message groups. — [JMIR Formative 2025](https://formative.jmir.org/2025/1/e70158)
- BabySparks users report friction at a required 5 activities per day. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/babysparks-development-app/id794574199)
- Vroom's design rule: the activity must need no extra time, money or equipment. — [Washington DOH](https://www.doh.wa.gov/CommunityandEnvironment/EssentialsforChildhoodInitiative/BrainBuildingwithVroom)
- Late-talker courses sold to parents are typically chunked as 5–8 weekly modules (e.g., "5 Weeks to More Talking"; an 8-week on-demand "Talk Together Online"; an 8-week parent programme at $97). — [Mary Barbera](https://marybarbera.com/5-weeks-to-more-talking/); [Talk Together](https://iwspeech.teachable.com/p/talk-together); [Skool listing](https://www.skool.com/amandateachestalking)

### Inferences
- A workable Todug unit: **one strategy card per week** (≤ 1 phone screen: why in 1–2 sentences, one "do this during [routine]" instruction, a 2–4 line example exchange), plus **one micro-variation per day** (a single line: same strategy, different routine or word target). This keeps the weekly cognitive load at one idea while still giving a daily reason to open.
- Todug's existing "3 parent steps + scripted dialogue" is close to the right size, but three steps per day × 28 days = 84 new instructions; the dosage evidence suggests repeating one strategy across a week rather than introducing new steps daily.
- The scripted parent/child dialogue is the text substitute for video modelling; keep it but make it shorter and clearly an *example*, since no source shows scripts beat principles.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures optimal reading time, word count or video length for parent-coaching content specifically; "1–3 minutes" is industry practice, not an evidenced threshold.
- No evidence found on text-script vs video modelling of the same strategy.

## What tracking mechanic best shows parents progress in language specifically?

### Takeaway
No head-to-head evidence exists, but the instruments clinicians and researchers actually use to register change in toddlers are parent-report vocabulary checklists (CDI-style word lists), and the behavioural trials show that feeding parents a number about their own effort (goal vs actual; LENA turn counts) changes behaviour. Streak mechanics raise next-day return in consumer apps but demotivate when broken, and there is no parent-specific evidence for them.

### Cited Findings

**Vocabulary checklist / word tracker**
- The MacArthur-Bates CDI is a parent-report vocabulary checklist authorised for adaptation in 117 language varieties besides English. — [Language Development Research (Tseltal checklist, 2025)](https://ldr.lps.library.cmu.edu/article/862/galley/655/view)
- A Mongolian (Khalkha) adaptation of the CDI Words & Sentences (toddler) form is listed as in development; norms and validity evidence are not yet available and it is not available for distribution. Draft sources: spontaneous-speech data from Mongolian-speaking children aged 18–48 months plus community focus groups. — [Stanford MB-CDI, Info2023-Mongolian](https://mb-cdi.stanford.edu/documents/Info2023-Mongolian.pdf)
- A published method for building CDI-style vocabulary checklists in new languages reports an adaptation process of about two weeks and validity for assessing early language. — [PMC5948920](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5948920); [CORE record](https://core.ac.uk/works/130587624)
- Commercial late-talker courses bundle "a vocabulary inventory and progress trackers" and self-assessments with lifetime access. — [TalkToddler course](https://carly-tulloch.squarespace.com/talktoddler)

**Milestone checklist + shareable summary**
- CDC Milestone Tracker (free, English/Spanish, 2 months–5 years): illustrated checklists with photos/videos per age, tips/activities, a "When to Act Early" section, a milestone summary that can be emailed to the child's doctor, and appointment/screening reminders; CDC states it does not collect identifying information. — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones-app/)
- BabySparks links each activity to a milestone so parents see what the day's activity is for; Kinedu premium includes progress reports. — [BabySparks App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/babysparks-development-app/id794574199); [Kinedu App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id741277284)

**Effort feedback (goal vs actual; objective counts)**
- PACT: weekly goal, daily reminder, end-of-week actual-vs-goal feedback and recognition more than doubled reading time (+88.3 min over 6 weeks). — [J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/node/2124)
- LENA Start: recording-based feedback on adult words and conversational turns reviewed weekly; significant between-group effects on child vocalisations and conversational turns in a small non-randomised study. — [PubMed 32921333](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32921333/)
- ParentText is testing "gamified module-completion updates" as an engagement component; result not found. — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05003518)

**Streaks — evidence and criticism**
- Duolingo reports learners reaching a 7-day streak are 2.4× more likely to return the next day, and a streak-wager test raised Day-7 retention by 14% **[marketing/company data]**; it also added forgiveness mechanics such as the Weekend Amulet. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit); [Duolingo blog – improving the streak](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)
- Silverman & Barasch (J. Consumer Research, 2023): streaks motivate, but a broken streak demotivates and can drive people away from the behaviour. — [Psychology Today summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/202306/how-broken-streaks-sap-motivation)
- Critique: streaks extend use past the point people would otherwise stop, and a fitness-app study in the British Journal of Health Psychology found the motivation produced was guilt-driven and extrinsic. — [The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/insights/consumer-insights/streak-creep-the-perils-of-too-much-gamification)

### Inferences
- For language specifically, the most legible progress artefact is a **"words my child says/understands" list that grows** (count + date of each new word, with a simple chart and a "first 10 / 25 / 50 words" marker). It is text-native, mirrors the CDI format, and doubles as the summary a parent can show a speech therapist or paediatrician — the CDC "share with your doctor" pattern.
- Because no normed Mongolian CDI exists, a Todug word list must be presented as a personal tracker, **not** a screening score; there is an opening to collaborate with the Mongolian CDI adaptation team rather than invent norms.
- Pair the outcome tracker (words) with an effort tracker (days practised vs the parent's own weekly goal). Late talkers progress slowly; an effort metric gives a win when the word count has not moved.
- If a streak is used at all, design it forgivingly (weekly targets such as "4 of 7 days", free "rest days", no loss framing). Tired parents of a delayed child are the audience most exposed to the guilt effect the literature describes. Nothing found supports punitive streaks for parents.
- A weekly 3-question check-in ("new words? new gestures/sounds? how often did you practise?") is the cheapest way to generate both personalisation data and a progress story.

### Gaps
- No study found comparing tracker types (word list vs milestone log vs streak vs video diary) on parent retention or child outcomes.
- No evidence found on certificates or video diaries in parenting products.
- Could not open the Mongolian CDI info sheet to confirm authors, institution or current (2026) status; the "in development" status is from a 2023 document.
- ASQ-3 integration patterns (e.g., Sparkler) were searched but not found.

## Which patterns are feasible for a text-first web app without video production budget?

### Takeaway
Almost all of the patterns with causal evidence are text-native (FACT/TIP/GROWTH messages, routine-anchored tips, goal + reminder + feedback, checklists); the patterns that require heavy investment (thousands of videos, hardware feedback, live clinicians) are the ones with the weakest or no independent evidence. The realistic differentiators for a small Mongolian-language team are delivery channel, routine anchoring, a word tracker, lightweight human touch, and conservative, expert-reviewed AI.

### Cited Findings

**Feasible, text-native patterns (see sections above for evidence)**
- FACT/TIP/GROWTH weekly triplet — 0.11 SD child literacy, 0.15–0.29 SD parent involvement. — [JHR 2019](https://jhr.uwpress.org/content/54/3/537.full.pdf)
- Goal-setting + reminders + weekly feedback — doubled parent activity. — [NBER w21602](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21602.pdf)
- Routine/play-embedded strategies — g = 0.50 expressive vocabulary subgroup. — [ASHA Evidence Map](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/2df79249-9828-4a65-9c01-ff8a849e7a39)
- "No time, money or equipment" + stated science per tip. — [Washington DOH on Vroom](https://www.doh.wa.gov/CommunityandEnvironment/EssentialsforChildhoodInitiative/BrainBuildingwithVroom)
- Low delivery cost at scale: ~£3.05 per parent for an 18-month text programme. — [Nesta](https://www.nesta.org.uk/report/engaging-parents-digitally-learning-from-the-tiny-happy-people-text-messaging-service/full/)

**Human touch / community**
- Hybrid telepractice (live coaching + remote) to teach caregivers EMT strategies showed preliminary efficacy. — [JSLHR 2021](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2021_JSLHR-20-00430)

### Inferences
Feasibility ranking for Todug (my synthesis; S/M/L = small-team effort):

| Pattern | Evidence strength | Effort | Fit for text-first web app |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly strategy as FACT → TIP → GROWTH, pushed 3×/week | Strong (RCTs) | S | Direct |
| Anchor every activity to a named routine; parent picks the routine (implementation intention) | Moderate (meta-analytic subgroup + PACT) | S | Direct |
| Weekly self-set goal + end-of-week "you did X of Y days" recap | Moderate (PACT RCT, different domain) | S | Extends existing tick-off |
| Child word list / "first 50 words" tracker with shareable summary | Indirect (CDI practice; no retention study) | S–M | Direct; avoid norm claims |
| Short onboarding quiz → starting level; weekly 3-question check-in → next week's strategy | Practice-based, no trial data | M | Direct |
| Red-flag checklist + "see a professional" screen + exportable summary | Standard practice (CDC) | S | Direct; high trust value |
| AI game generator constrained to this week's strategy, child's interests and a chosen routine; refusal/redirect on medical or diagnostic questions; SLP-reviewed prompt and sample audits | Practice-based; risk literature is cautionary | M | Already partly built |
| Messenger/Viber/SMS delivery of nudges | Strong for SMS; chatbot evidence mixed | M | Web push first; check platform rules on messaging non-responders |
| Monthly live Q&A with an SLP / cohort chat group | Weak-to-moderate (feasibility studies; LENA group graduation) | M (recurring human cost) | Good premium tier |
| Asynchronous video feedback from an SLP | Weak (single-case) | M–L | Premium add-on only |
| Library of filmed modelling videos | No independent evidence for app libraries | L | Skip; reuse a few phone-shot clips or illustrated scripts |

- Monetisation: Todug's "week 1 free, then pay" is a 7-day trial in front of a hard paywall. RevenueCat's data says hard paywalls convert far better than freemium but longer trials convert better than short ones; these are app-store subscription benchmarks and may not transfer to a bank-transfer, one-time-plan product. The late-talker course market ($97–$298, one-time, lifetime access, sometimes with a bonus call) is the closer comparison than baby-app subscriptions ($7–12/month). A one-time "4-week plan" price with an optional human-touch upgrade matches that market.
- The free week should contain the patterns most likely to produce a felt win: the starting word list, the first routine-anchored strategy, and a recap at day 7 — so the paywall arrives right after visible progress.
- Because bank transfer adds friction at the conversion moment, the day-7 recap ("you practised 5 of 7 days; 2 new words") is also the natural payment prompt.
- Localisation: the literature's warning is that most adaptations stop at translation. For Mongolian this argues for original word lists (not translated English first-word lists), local routines (e.g., ger/apartment life, tea, grandparents as co-caregivers), and extended-family framing as in Thrive by Five.
- Clinical review: no product found publishes a detailed review protocol; the visible norm is "created/reviewed by SLPs", "not a diagnosis / not a substitute for therapy", and an explicit escalation path. Naming a Mongolian SLP reviewer and dating the review would exceed the norm.

### Gaps
- No public conversion data for any parent-coaching product (trial-to-paid, course completion). RevenueCat category medians for Education/Health were not retrievable.
- No evidence on Viber/Messenger specifically (the dominant channels in Mongolia are an assumption to check); ParentText evidence is WhatsApp-centred.
- No documented AI-activity-generator feature with published guardrails was found in an early-language product; Huckleberry's and Kinedu's descriptions are company statements and Kinedu's AI details could not be opened.
- Nothing found on Mongolian-language adaptations of Hanen, Care for Child Development materials in Mongolia, or Mongolian SLP workforce numbers; Bebbo has no confirmed Mongolian edition.
- Pricing for the best-known late-talker creator courses (Raising Little Talkers, Speech Sisters) was not returned by search.
