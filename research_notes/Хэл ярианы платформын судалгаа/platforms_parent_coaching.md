# International parent-facing speech & language platforms for late-talking toddlers/preschoolers (state as of Nov 2026)

> Method note (read first): In this session direct page fetching was blocked for almost every product domain (toddlertalk.com, apps.apple.com, expressable.com, teachable, bbc.co.uk etc. were outside the allowed network). All findings below therefore come from web-search result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading full pages. Prices and counts are as reported by those pages/snippets; several conflict between sources and are flagged. Anything not confirmable is listed under Gaps rather than asserted.

## 1. For each product: exact content structure and lesson format — what does one lesson/activity look like to a parent?

### Takeaway
Products fall into four formats: (a) child-facing peer-video apps (Speech Blubs, Otsimo), (b) parent-facing "assessment → custom lesson plan" tools (Toddler Talk, SpeakEasy — the closest analogues to Todug), (c) one-off SLP-made video courses of roughly 2–2.5 hours cut into 20–32 short lessons (<10 min each) plus a PDF workbook (Speech Sisters, Wee Talkers, Raising Little Talkers, Tiny Talkers, Walkie Talkie), and (d) general child-development "daily plan" apps where language is one of several domains (Kinedu, BabySparks, Pathfinder, Lovevery). None of the sources I could access shows a competitor using a scripted parent/child dialogue as a standard lesson element — that appears to be a Todug differentiator (unverified because full lesson samples were not readable).

### Cited Findings

**A. Parent-facing assessment → plan tools (closest to Todug)**
- Toddler Talk (toddlertalk.com) is active: it offers a free online "Toddler Talk Assessment" and a customized at-home activity plan built from it; the lesson list covers gestures, imitating sounds and words, first words, two-word phrases and following directions — [Toddler Talk program page](https://toddlertalk.com/toddler-talk-language-program)
- Toddler Talk's plan is split into two lesson types: "speech therapy strategy lessons" (e.g. "How asking less questions can help your toddler learn to talk") and "participation lessons" (e.g. supporting attention span, learning the toddler's individual communication signs); the site names focused stimulation as one of its core word-teaching strategies — [Toddler Talk free lesson](https://toddlertalk.com/free-lesson); [Toddler Talk program page](https://toddlertalk.com/toddler-talk-language-program)
- Toddler Talk also publishes free monthly milestone checklists for 12–36 months and a separate parent "Pronunciation Profile" articulation screener — [Toddler Talk home](https://toddlertalk.com/); [Articulation screener](https://toddlertalk.com/blog/articulation-screener)
- SpeakEasy: Home Speech Therapy (app, created by a speech therapist) is parent-facing: screening questions suggest a starting level, then the app guides the caregiver through activities at that level; a reviewer describes four broad levels; it has routine-based at-home activities, word tracking, skill tracking and in-app games (Space Match, Sound Check, Splat). Target users: parents of children 0–5, including typically developing, in therapy, autistic, or with learning delays — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/speakeasy-home-speech-therapy/id1511838622); [EducationalAppStore review](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/speakeasy-home-speech-therapy); [Toddler Talk's review of apps](https://toddlertalk.com/blog/apps-for-toddlers-learning-to-talk)

**B. Child-facing peer-video apps**
- Speech Blubs (Blub Blub Inc.; active): content is organised into themed "sections" (the App Store listing says 28, e.g. Early Sounds, Animal Kingdom, Mouth Gym; a Mom's Choice listing says 25 sections with 1,500+ activities). Real children act as "video models" that the child imitates — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1239522573); [Mom's Choice Awards](https://store.momschoiceawards.com/product/9621)
- Speech Blubs is browsed by theme together with the child; the parent menu holds reminders, daily progress and the personalized report. Company-recommended age 2–5 (a review site says 1–8) — [Speech Blubs user guide](https://speechblubs.com/blog/speech-blubs-a-users-guide)
- Otsimo Speech Therapy (active): 15+ categories (First Words, Basic Shapes…), 200+ exercises; peer "kid model" videos, mirror-like camera filters for role play, speech recognition that the developer says adapts the games; a "Pathway" runs from oral-motor exercises (jaw, tongue, lips) through sound imitation to simple sentences. Ages 0–8 per KidSafe — [Otsimo user guide](https://otsimo.com/en/otsimo-speech-therapy-user-guide/); [Otsimo FAQ](https://www.otsimo.com/en/frequently-asked-questions/); [KidSafe listing](https://kidsafe.com/member/speechtherapy_app)
- Articulation Station has been repackaged as "Articulation Station Hive", sold through a Little Bee Speech "Hive" membership that also includes Articulation Test Center Hive (Pro tier). It is an articulation (speech-sound) drill tool, not an early-language programme — [App Store: Articulation Station Hive](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1485607474)

**C. SLP-made parent video courses (one-time purchase)**
- Speech Sisters (two SLPs who are parents) sell age-banded self-paced courses: Talk on Track (babies 0–14 months), Time to Talk (toddlers 15–36 months, "for late talkers"), and Tiny Articulators (speech sounds); plus paid virtual consultations and a free "Does my child need speech therapy?" webinar. Most Time to Talk lessons are under 10 minutes and strategies are meant to fit into everyday routines — [Time to Talk](https://www.speechsisters.com/time-to-talk); [Talk on Track](https://www.speechsisters.com/talk-on-track); [Tiny Articulators](https://www.speechsisters.com/tiny-articulators)
- Wee Talkers TalkToddler: one listing describes 7 core modules, 30 short video lessons, a 45-page PDF, a full milestones list, a toddler sign-language tutorial and 3 daily-routine guides; another Wee Talkers page says "all 5 TalkToddler modules (about 2.5 hours of content)" — the module count is inconsistent across their own pages. Lifetime access. Wee Talkers also sells a talkbaby course and a "Songs + Stories" membership (songs, rhymes, storytime videos, monthly class) — [Wee Talkers TalkToddler](https://www.weetalkers.com/talktoddler); [Wee Talkers store](https://courses.weetalkers.com/store); [Wee Talkers FAQ](https://www.weetalkers.com/faqs)
- Raising Little Talkers (Melissa Minney, SLP): signature course described as a ~2-hour video course in bite-size lessons (7 sections / 32 lessons per the Circle listing); the current course page lists modules starting with "Module 1: The Language Building Blocks" and covers imitating words and phrases and combining words. Separate Speech Sounds course and a free workshop for parents of toddlers under 3 — [Course page](https://www.raisinglittletalkers.com/course); [Circle listing](https://discover.circle.so/products/raising-little-talkers); [Free workshop](https://learn.raisinglittletalkers.com/workshop/)
- "Tiny Talkers" is an ambiguous name used by several unrelated small businesses: (1) Tiny Talkers (Chicago) — online "Let's Get Talking" course plus 6-week in-person caregiver-and-child classes for 15–36 months; (2) Tiny Talkers Academy (tinytalkersspeech.com) — ~2 hours of content in 22 modules (page still shows placeholder "$xxxx" values, so it looks unfinished); (3) SpeechRise "Tiny Talker Playbook" — six modules — [Tiny Talkers Chicago course](https://www.tinytalkerschicago.com/courseinfo); [Tiny Talkers Academy](https://www.tinytalkersspeech.com/); [Tiny Talker Playbook](https://flower-herring-a2lc.squarespace.com/tiny-talker-playbook)
- Walkie Talkie Speech Therapy (Kayla Chalko): 10-week online course "How to Teach a Toddler to Talk" for parents of children 12 months–5 years (late talkers and autistic children), with a printable workbook containing assignments, learning activities and progress tracking, plus a private Facebook community and weekly free YouTube tips — [Walkie Talkie Speech Therapy](https://walkietalkiespeechtherapy.com/); [Courses shop](https://shop.walkietalkiespeechtherapy.com/collections/courses)
- Teach Me To Talk (Laura Mize, SLP; active): content is mainly therapy manuals, a podcast and courses rather than an app. Flagship framework is the "11 prelinguistic skills" toddlers need before words emerge, from the manual *Let's Talk About Talking* (2nd ed.), with an "11 Skills Chart" (also in Spanish) and a newer "11 Prelinguistic Skills Every Late Talking Toddler Needs" course. She has authored 9 manuals — [Let's Talk About Talking](https://teachmetotalk.com/product/lets-talk-about-talking/); [11 Skills Chart (Spanish)](https://teachmetotalk.com/product/11-skills-chart-en-espanol/); [SpeechTherapyPD profile](https://app.speechtherapypd.com/presenters/Laura-Mize)

**D. Therapy services with a home-practice layer**
- Expressable (US teletherapy; active): its client portal shows the care plan, progress toward goals and session notes, and the therapist assigns "Learning Paths" explaining the strategies covered in sessions — with demo videos, home-practice tips and real-life examples — plus weekly home practice activities — [Expressable portal article](https://expressable.com/learning-center/expressable/our-expressable-portal-helps-you-make-faster-progress-in-speech-therapy)
- Mable Therapy (UK; active): live online sessions with HCPC/RCSLT-registered therapists using built-in games and activities; parents get access to the software to reinforce at home; typically recommends an initial block of 6–10 weeks — [Mable pricing](https://www.mabletherapy.com/speech-pricing); [Mable help: cost](https://help.mabletherapy.com/knowledge/how-much-does-therapy-cost)

**E. General development apps (language is one domain)**
- Kinedu: onboarding milestone questionnaire → daily plan (4 activities per day per the parents' manual) delivered as step-by-step activity videos; plan runs 7 days, then a new plan is generated from achieved/missing milestones — [Kinedu 101](https://www.kinedu.com/blog/kinedu-101); [Kinedu parents manual](https://www.kinedu.com/docs/kinedu-parents-manual.html)
- BabySparks: personalized daily programme of activities by age/stage, organised by domain (cognitive, language/speech, gross motor, fine motor, social-emotional, sensory, self-care) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/babysparks-development-app/id794574199)
- Pathfinder Health: milestone tracking aligned to AAP guidance with personalised tips/activities; a third-party profile cites 1,000+ screen-free activities and 600+ articles, and built-in screeners (M-CHAT, SWYC) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1529426446); [Dealroom profile](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/pathfinder_health_1)
- Lovevery app: stage-based activities and expert advice tied to the child's stage (birth to ~3+), bundled with Play Kit subscriptions — [Lovevery blog](https://blog.lovevery.com/product-recommendations/the-lovevery-app-for-parents/)

**F. Free public resources**
- BBC Tiny Happy People (free, under-5s incl. pregnancy): hundreds of activities filterable by child age; each age band opens with a "Tools for Talking" film modelling one technique; each activity has a short video for parents plus an explanation of how it boosts language; also has child-development and tips/advice sections — [Solgrid guide](https://www.solgrid.org.uk/eyc/?p=6499); [Royal Greenwich](https://www.royalgreenwich.gov.uk/whats-new-our-local-offer/2025/bbc-tiny-happy-people)
- CDC Milestone Tracker (free, iOS/Android, English and Spanish): illustrated interactive milestone checklists from 2 months to 5 years with photos/videos of milestones, prematurity adjustment, tips and activities, appointment reminders and a summary to share with the doctor — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones-app/)
- Speech and Language UK "Child's progress checker": free online; parent picks child's age and answers questions; covers 0–7 years per a council listing; explicitly not a specialist assessment — [Progress checker](https://progress-checker.speechandlanguage.org.uk/); [Bradford guide](https://fyi.bradford.gov.uk/speech-and-language-content-for-early-years/screen-and-assess)

**G. Added reference point (clinician-led, strongest evidence)**
- Hanen "It Takes Two to Talk": parent programme led by a Hanen-certified SLP with a parent guidebook; parents learn three clusters of responsive interaction strategies — [Hanen research summary](https://hanen.org/about-us/who-we-are-research/it-takes-two-to-talk-research-summary)

### Inferences
- The dominant course format is "micro-lesson video (<10 min) + PDF workbook + routine guides"; total runtime is consistently ~2–2.5 hours. Todug's text-plus-dialogue lessons are lighter-weight; short modelling video is the main media gap versus the field.
- Toddler Talk's split into "strategy lessons" (what the parent does) and "participation lessons" (attention, engagement prerequisites) mirrors Todug's stage 1 (joint attention/turn-taking) vs later stages and is worth studying directly.
- Routine-embedded practice ("daily routine guides", "routine-based activities") is a near-universal element, validating Todug's daily-routine ideas.

### Gaps
- Could not read any full sample lesson, so the exact on-screen anatomy of a lesson (steps, scripts, examples) for Toddler Talk, SpeakEasy, Speech Sisters, Wee Talkers, Raising Little Talkers is unverified.
- Full module/lesson title lists for Time to Talk, Talk on Track, TalkToddler and Raising Little Talkers were not retrievable.
- Lovevery app and Pathfinder activity format details (length, video vs text) not confirmed.
- "Chatterboxes": no parent-facing product of that name could be verified in search; treat as unverified (it may be a local clinic rather than a consumer platform).
- Khan Academy Kids was not researched (not a speech/language coaching product).
- ASHA's parent milestone materials were not retrieved in this session.

## 2. How do they sequence skills (developmental ladder? by age? by goal?) and decide when a child moves on?

### Takeaway
Three sequencing models exist: by age band (Speech Sisters, Wee Talkers, BBC, CDC, Lovevery), by developmental skill ladder/level (Toddler Talk, SpeakEasy, Teach Me To Talk's 11 prelinguistic skills, Otsimo "Pathway"), and by theme with no real ladder (Speech Blubs). Explicit, published "move-on" mastery criteria were not found for any consumer product; the nearest mechanisms are Kinedu's weekly re-planning from parent-reported milestones and SpeakEasy's skill tracking.

### Cited Findings
- Speech Sisters sequence by age: Talk on Track 0–14 months, Time to Talk 15–36 months, then Tiny Articulators for speech sounds — [Talk on Track](https://www.speechsisters.com/talk-on-track); [Time to Talk](https://www.speechsisters.com/time-to-talk)
- Wee Talkers likewise separates talkbaby and TalkToddler courses — [Wee Talkers store](https://courses.weetalkers.com/store)
- Toddler Talk's lesson topics follow a developmental order — gestures → imitating sounds/words → first words → two-word phrases, plus following directions (receptive) — and the plan is chosen from assessment results rather than age alone — [Toddler Talk program page](https://toddlertalk.com/toddler-talk-language-program)
- SpeakEasy uses broad levels (a reviewer counts four) with a screener-suggested starting level, and parents can also choose the level — [EducationalAppStore](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/speakeasy-home-speech-therapy); [Toddler Talk app review](https://toddlertalk.com/blog/apps-for-toddlers-learning-to-talk)
- Laura Mize's ladder is explicitly pre-verbal: 11 prelinguistic skills. Those confirmed from her site: #1 responds to/interacts with environmental events and objects, #2 responds to people, #6 develops appropriate play skills, #7 understands words and follows directions ("kids must understand words before they can use them"), #11 initiates interaction with others — [Skill #1](https://teachmetotalk.com/2021/05/26/386-prelinguistic-skill-1-teach-a-child-to-respond-and-interact-with-environmental-events/); [Skill #2](https://teachmetotalk.com/2019/10/13/387-prelinguistic-skill-2-teach-a-child-to-respond-to-people/); [Skill #11](https://teachmetotalk.com/2021/05/26/396-prelinguistic-skill-11-initiates-with-others/); [11 Skills podcast series](https://teachmetotalk.com/2020/03/26/11-skills-podcast-series-ce-credit-available/)
- Raising Little Talkers starts with "The Language Building Blocks" and progresses to imitating words/phrases and combining words — [Course page](https://www.raisinglittletalkers.com/course)
- Otsimo's "Pathway" moves from oral-motor exercises to sound imitation to simple sentences — [Otsimo user guide](https://otsimo.com/en/otsimo-speech-therapy-user-guide/)
- Speech Blubs is organised by themed sections rather than a staged ladder — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1239522573)
- Kinedu advances weekly: after each activity the parent marks related milestones as achieved or not; at the end of the 7-day plan the app analyses completed/missing milestones and generates the next plan, mixing easy and more challenging activities — [Kinedu 101](https://www.kinedu.com/blog/kinedu-101); [Kinedu parents manual](https://www.kinedu.com/docs/kinedu-parents-manual.html)
- BBC Tiny Happy People and the CDC app are organised purely by age; CDC checklists run 2 months–5 years — [Solgrid guide](https://www.solgrid.org.uk/eyc/?p=6499); [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones-app/)
- In the Hanen ITTT research, each child was assigned 10 developmentally appropriate target words based on interests and phonemic repertoire (Girolametto, Pearce & Weitzman 1996) — [Hanen research summary](https://hanen.org/about-us/who-we-are-research/it-takes-two-to-talk-research-summary)

### Inferences
- Todug's 11-stage ladder is more granular on the verbal side (request words, verbs, 2-word, 3-word, questions, narrative) than any consumer product found; competitors are comparatively stronger on the pre-verbal side (Mize's 11 prelinguistic skills cover responding to environment/people, play, comprehension, initiation — areas Todug compresses into one "joint attention & turn-taking" stage).
- Explicit "signs of mastery" per stage appear to be rare in consumer products; Kinedu's "did your child do X after this activity?" micro-check is the most reusable mechanic for deciding progression.
- Interest- and sound-repertoire-based target-word selection (Hanen) is an evidence-backed personalization lever Todug already partly has via "interests".

### Gaps
- The full ordered list of Mize's 11 skills (#3–5, #8–10) was not retrieved.
- No source gave explicit advancement criteria for Toddler Talk, SpeakEasy, Speech Blubs or the SLP courses.
- Names/definitions of SpeakEasy's levels not confirmed.

## 3. What onboarding assessment do they use, and how does it map to content?

### Takeaway
A free parent-report milestone questionnaire that ends in a personalised report is the standard acquisition funnel (Toddler Talk ~10 min; Speech Blubs 20+ questions; SpeakEasy screening questions; Kinedu milestone survey), and it doubles as the placement mechanism. Video courses use no assessment — they place by age only. Validated screeners (M-CHAT, SWYC) are used only by Pathfinder.

### Cited Findings
- Toddler Talk Assessment: free, about 10 minutes, compares the child with same-age peers across communication areas, outputs a detailed milestone summary and drives the customized at-home plan — [Toddler Talk program page](https://toddlertalk.com/toddler-talk-language-program); [Evaluation blog](https://toddlertalk.com/blog/does-my-toddler-need-speech-therapy)
- Toddler Talk positions the tool as a complement to — not a replacement for — a formal 1–2 hour evaluation by a licensed speech therapist — [Evaluation blog](https://toddlertalk.com/blog/does-my-toddler-need-speech-therapy)
- Speech Blubs onboarding asks 20+ questions on the child's milestones (hearing, pragmatics, play, comprehension, talking) and generates a personalised report with background on each area, warning signs, and tips for using the app; the quiz can be retaken later to see progress. The company frames it as yielding a "personalized learning plan" — [screensdesign breakdown](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/speech-blubs-language-therapy); [Homeschooling4Him review](https://homeschooling4him.com/speech-blubs-review-speech-therapy-app-for-toddlers/); [Speech Blubs screener](https://the.speechblubs.com/base/screener-intro)
- Critique: an App Store reviewer says Speech Blubs does not actually provide individualised assessment, and a UX teardown calls the onboarding very thorough but very long — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/speech-blubs-language-therapy/id1239522573); [screensdesign breakdown](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/speech-blubs-language-therapy)
- SpeakEasy: screening questions suggest where to start, then activities are served for that level — [Toddler Talk app review](https://toddlertalk.com/blog/apps-for-toddlers-learning-to-talk)
- Kinedu: initial milestone questionnaire (skippable) establishes reached vs in-progress milestones and tailors the activity plan — [Kinedu 101](https://www.kinedu.com/blog/kinedu-101)
- Pathfinder Health incorporates clinically validated screening tools such as M-CHAT (autism) and SWYC — [Dealroom profile](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/pathfinder_health_1)
- CDC states its app is not a substitute for validated, standardized developmental screening tools — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones-app/)
- Speech and Language UK progress checker: choose age, answer questions; differs from a specialist assessment — [Progress checker](https://progress-checker.speechandlanguage.org.uk/)
- Expressable and Mable are placed by a clinician evaluation/care plan rather than a self-serve quiz — [Expressable portal article](https://expressable.com/learning-center/expressable/our-expressable-portal-helps-you-make-faster-progress-in-speech-therapy); [Mable help](https://help.mabletherapy.com/knowledge/how-much-does-therapy-cost)

### Inferences
- The report itself (peer comparison + warning signs + next steps) is treated as a product deliverable and marketing hook, not just a routing step; a re-takeable assessment gives a cheap progress measure.
- All credible products add a "not a diagnosis / see an SLP" disclaimer; Todug should do the same and include red-flag referral guidance.

### Gaps
- Exact question counts/areas of the Toddler Talk Assessment and SpeakEasy screener, and the mapping rules from answers to lessons, are not public in what I could access.

## 4. What media do they use (short video modelling, peer video, audio, printable, text scripts)?

### Takeaway
Video is the default medium everywhere: peer-child modelling video for child-facing apps, and short adult demonstration/explainer video for parent-facing products, nearly always paired with printables (PDF workbooks, checklists, charts). Text-first delivery is rare; text-message delivery (BBC pilot) is the notable low-bandwidth alternative.

### Cited Findings
- Peer video modelling: Speech Blubs (real children as "video models") and Otsimo (pronunciation-checked "kid models", plus mirror-like face filters and speech recognition) — [App Store: Speech Blubs](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1239522573); [Otsimo user guide](https://otsimo.com/en/otsimo-speech-therapy-user-guide/)
- Short parent-facing video lessons + PDF: Wee Talkers (30 short videos, 45-page PDF, sign-language tutorial, 3 routine guides) — [Wee Talkers TalkToddler](https://www.weetalkers.com/talktoddler); Speech Sisters (most lessons <10 min) — [Time to Talk](https://www.speechsisters.com/time-to-talk); Raising Little Talkers (~2 h video in bite-size lessons) — [Circle listing](https://discover.circle.so/products/raising-little-talkers)
- Printable workbook with assignments and progress tracking + Facebook community + YouTube: Walkie Talkie — [Walkie Talkie Speech Therapy](https://walkietalkiespeechtherapy.com/)
- Manuals, charts, podcast (audio) and YouTube: Teach Me To Talk — [Let's Talk About Talking](https://teachmetotalk.com/product/lets-talk-about-talking/); [11 Skills podcast series](https://teachmetotalk.com/2020/03/26/11-skills-podcast-series-ce-credit-available/)
- Demo videos + tips + real-life examples inside clinician-assigned Learning Paths: Expressable — [Expressable portal article](https://expressable.com/learning-center/expressable/our-expressable-portal-helps-you-make-faster-progress-in-speech-therapy)
- Step-by-step activity videos: Kinedu — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kinedu-baby-development/id741277284)
- Short films modelling a technique + activity videos: BBC Tiny Happy People; also delivered via a text-messaging programme piloted with Nesta — [Solgrid guide](https://www.solgrid.org.uk/eyc/?p=6499); [Nesta](https://www.nesta.org.uk/project/bbc-tiny-happy-people-text-messaging-programme/)
- Milestone photos and videos: CDC Milestone Tracker — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones-app/)
- In-app child games plus off-screen at-home activities: SpeakEasy — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/speakeasy-home-speech-therapy/id1511838622)
- Live video sessions with built-in games: Mable Therapy — [Mable pricing](https://www.mabletherapy.com/speech-pricing)
- Songs, rhymes and storytime videos as a recurring-membership content type: Wee Talkers — [Wee Talkers FAQ](https://www.weetalkers.com/faqs)

### Inferences
- A short (≈1 min) adult-modelling clip per strategy is the cheapest way for Todug to reach parity; Kinedu's Stanford-linked claim (one short video changed parent behaviour — see Q6) and the BBC RCT both support short video specifically.
- Songs/rhymes (Wee Talkers) are a content type absent from Todug's current lesson format and culturally easy to localise for Mongolian.

### Gaps
- Typical video durations for Kinedu, BBC and Wee Talkers not confirmed.
- Whether Toddler Talk lessons are video, text or both was not confirmed.

## 5. Pricing and free-tier design

### Takeaway
Free assessment/report + paid plan or subscription is the norm for apps (roughly US$7–28/month, US$60–160/year); SLP video courses are one-time purchases of roughly US$69–297 with lifetime access; therapy services charge per session. Prices vary by source, country and promotion, so all figures below are indicative.

## 6. Any research/RCT/pilot evidence behind the product

### Takeaway
No consumer app or paid course in this set has an independent peer-reviewed efficacy trial that I could find; their claims rest on general literature (video modelling, parent-implemented intervention) and testimonials. The only product-specific controlled evidence found is for BBC Tiny Happy People (university-run RCT of video text messages) and for the clinician-led Hanen programme; Kinedu cites Stanford-linked studies of parent behaviour that I could not trace to a paper.

### Cited Findings
- Speech Blubs: efficacy claims rely on the general video-modelling literature (its own "Proof of Concept" literature review cites e.g. Charlop & Milstein 1989) and a "UCLA study published by ASHA" that company materials do not cite verifiably; no independent trial of the app was found — [Speech Blubs proof-of-concept PDF](https://cdn.speechblubs.com/2023/07/Design-Proof-of-Concept_compressed-1.pdf); [Speech Blubs video modelling blog](https://speechblubs.com/blog/teaching-kids-with-video-modeling/)
- Otsimo: claims "scientifically proven peer modelling" and ABA-style repetition; vendor claims only, no independent outcome study found — [Otsimo user guide](https://otsimo.com/en/otsimo-speech-therapy-user-guide/)
- SpeakEasy: describes activities as evidence-based and written by speech therapists, used by 100,000+ parents and professionals; no study of the app found — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/speakeasy-home-speech-therapy/id1511838622)
- Speech Sisters, Raising Little Talkers, Walkie Talkie: evidence is seller-published testimonials (e.g. "3 words to well over 200 in 4 months"; "850+ families enrolled in 2.5 years"); courses are described as based on research-supported SLP strategies — [Time to Talk](https://www.speechsisters.com/time-to-talk); [Walkie Talkie Speech Therapy](https://walkietalkiespeechtherapy.com/); [Raising Little Talkers older checkout](https://course.raisinglittletalkers.com/checkout-a)
- Expressable: outcomes page is mainly testimonials; its blog cites third-party research on parent-implemented intervention (a 2022 J. Autism Dev. Disord. study; a Vanderbilt study), not studies of Expressable's portal — [Expressable outcomes](https://expressable.com/outcomes); [Expressable blog](https://expressable.com/learning-center/autism/new-research-parent-involvement-helps-autistic-children-with-communication)
- Kinedu: its science page claims that in a study with Stanford one short Kinedu activity video led parents to make 69% more bids for joint attention and speak 35% more; it also cites an analysis of 21,861 children's data ("The Structure of Developmental Variation in Early Childhood") — observational, not an RCT; MIT Solve describes small-scale tests in the US and Mexico — [Kinedu science](https://www.kinedu.com/science); [MIT Solve](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/7799)
- Kinedu marketing also claims "12%–21% more development in all areas" for users — self-reported figure — [Kinedu free trial page](https://courses.kinedu.com/kinedu-free-trial)
- Pathfinder Health: marketed as evidence-based/clinically validated via embedded screeners; no independent outcome study found — [Dealroom profile](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/pathfinder_health_1)
- BBC Tiny Happy People: an RCT led by Prof. Danielle Matthews (Sheffield, with Manchester, Liverpool, Oxford, Melbourne; BBC-funded) found parents sent THP language-learning videos by text showed more linguistic responsiveness than controls sent physical-health videos — [Nesta project](https://www.nesta.org.uk/project/bbc-tiny-happy-people-text-messaging-programme/); trial registration — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04919343](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04919343)
- BBC's written evidence to Parliament says interim longitudinal findings showed repeated use of THP films had a tangible impact on language acquisition at 18 months (broadcaster's own summary) — [UK Parliament written evidence](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/125450/pdf/)
- Nesta's learning report on the THP text service found more work is needed to reach the most disadvantaged parents; a 31-parent diary study found most were highly engaged — [Nesta report](https://www.nesta.org.uk/report/engaging-parents-digitally-learning-from-the-tiny-happy-people-text-messaging-service/full/)
- A 2026 IJLCD paper studied acceptability of a digital early-years language support service for caregivers of children with SLCN and/or multilingual families, noting earlier evaluations using THP materials had excluded these groups — [Hancock et al. 2026, IJLCD](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1460-6984.70210)
- Hanen It Takes Two to Talk: randomized delayed-treatment-control study (Girolametto, Pearce & Weitzman 1996) reported accelerated vocabulary and language development; later reviews judge the evidence "promising" but mixed across outcomes — [Hanen research summary](https://hanen.org/about-us/who-we-are-research/it-takes-two-to-talk-research-summary); [UCL case study review](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/educational-psychology/resources/CS1Bird22-25.pdf); [AJSLP 2025 review](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2025_AJSLP-25-00101)
- CDC explicitly disclaims its app as a substitute for validated screening — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones-app/)

### Inferences
- The evidence-backed "active ingredient" across the field is parent responsiveness coaching (Hanen, THP RCT, parent-implemented intervention literature), not app screen time for the child; Todug's parent-mediated design sits on the better-evidenced side.
- Because competitors lack product-level evidence, even a small pre/post pilot (e.g. parent-reported vocabulary checklist before and after the 4-week plan) would be a genuine differentiator for Todug.
- Honest positioning ("based on evidence-informed strategies; not a replacement for an SLP") matches what the more credible players do.
